## Abstract

Cookie-consent interfaces are often assessed from a single banner or
screenshot, which cannot establish whether refusal becomes easier, clearer, or
technically effective over time. This project develops a traceable
longitudinal audit framework with four components: path availability, path
effort, transparency, and choice effectiveness. The framework is applied in
two separate evidence lanes. A controlled five-site pilot tests whether web
captures, scoring context, and versioned evidence can support comparable
observations. Because the pilot contains only one validated interval, all five
local directional labels remain insufficient evidence. A purposively selected
historical case series reconstructs six dated company trajectories from
primary regulatory decisions, regulator follow-ups, and one company
announcement. Five cases improve at least one audited component and one later
regresses. In all three first-layer cases, Google, Facebook, and TikTok move
from acceptance-favoring rejection effort to one-click or equivalently simple
refusal, although TikTok's purpose information remains deficient. Orange and
SHEIN document technical remediation of refusal or withdrawal, while Vanity
Fair demonstrates that a later functional failure can follow an earlier closed
proceeding. These selected cases do not estimate web-wide prevalence or prove
that regulation alone caused every change. They show that concrete
interactions that can be specified and verified are the clearest site of
documented improvement, while consent quality remains component-specific,
partial, and reversible.
