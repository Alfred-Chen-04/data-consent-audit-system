# SSRP Consent Research Rehearsal Script, 2026-07-30

## 90-Second Poster Pitch

This project asks how the same cookie-consent interface changes over time. RQ1
defines a stable ruler: can users reach reject and customize, how much effort
does each path require, what is disclosed, and whether refusal actually stops
tracking. RQ2 preserves dated versions so those components can be compared.

The local five-site pilot validates the capture and comparison workflow, but it
has only one usable interval, so I do not claim local improvement or regression.
To recover a defensible longitudinal result, I added six company trajectories
with dated primary regulatory or company sources.

Five cases improved at least one component. Google, Facebook, and TikTok moved
rejection toward first-layer parity. Orange and SHEIN improved technical respect
for refusal or withdrawal. Vanity Fair later showed a functional regression
after an earlier proceeding had closed.

The main finding is not that cookie consent steadily improves. Concrete actions
that regulators can specify and check tend to improve first, but clearer buttons
can coexist with weak information or backend tracking, and systems can regress.
That raises the question of whether consent should be certified once or audited
continuously.

## Likely Questions

**Is this an experiment?**

No. It is a controlled method pilot plus a purposively selected observational
case series. The sources support dated direction, not randomized causation.

**Why only six companies?**

Each included case had to provide named, dated before/after evidence with enough
detail to code at least one component. The case series explains mechanisms; it
does not estimate prevalence.

**Can you say regulation caused the changes?**

Only at graded strength. Google directly attributed its redesign; Facebook and
Orange have order-and-follow-up evidence; TikTok and SHEIN changed during
investigations or proceedings; Vanity Fair's regression cause is unknown.

**Why keep the weak local pilot?**

It demonstrates the capture, versioning, and evidence-validation method and
shows why technical differences must not automatically become directional
claims.

**What would the next study do?**

Collect at least three controlled time points per site, freeze context and
scorer versions, double-code a subset, and re-audit technical refusal as well as
visible button parity.
